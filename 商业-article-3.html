LLMs Fail Transitivity Test in Moral Value Ranking: Aggregation Masks Instability

11/11/2026, 4:29:11 AM

📌 一句话摘要

A recent study finds that large language models show inconsistent moral rankings when option order changes， with 26 of 30 test sets showing unstable rankings， and aggregate analysis creates a false sense of consistent value hierarchies.

📝 详细摘要

The tweet discusses a University of Missouri study titled 'Do large language models rank moral values? A transitivity test of moral competence' by Katelyn Wasson and Kiwon Song. It explains that when users ask LLMs to rank moral options and then reverse the order， answers often change - suggesting models may simply pick the first option rather than genuinely compare. Testing 30 scenarios， 26 showed ranking instability. The paper warns that averaging model responses creates 'aggregation illusions' where 4 out of 5 models appear to have consistent ranking systems that don't match individual test results. The key insight is that good output scores don't guarantee stable value hierarchies， and single-instance consistency must precede claiming moral competence.

💡 主要观点

  1. LLM moral rankings fail transitivity test When option order is reversed in moral dilemmas， model answers frequently change， indicating models may choose first options without genuine comparison. 26 out of 30 test sets showed ranking instability， suggesting no stable value hierarchy exists.
  2. Aggregation creates false consistency illusion Averaging model responses across multiple tests can produce apparent consistent ranking systems even when individual model outputs are inconsistent， creating what the paper terms 'aggregation illusion' - a statistical artifact rather than genuine moral competence.
  3. Output quality ≠ moral capability Good-looking output scores don't necessarily indicate stable value hierarchies or genuine moral reasoning. True moral competence requires consistent single-instance decision-making， which this study finds lacking in most models.
