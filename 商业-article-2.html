Mixture of Experts: Scaling AI Models Without Scaling Compute

11/7/2026, 4:45:13 AM

📌 一句话摘要

This post explains how Mixture of Experts (MoE) allows scaling model parameters from 13B to 671B while only increasing per-token compute slightly， enabling larger models with controlled inference costs.

📝 详细摘要

The post introduces Mixture of Experts (MoE) as a solution to the scaling challenge in large language models， where parameter count increases but compute per token remains manageable. It explains the mechanism: a router selects top-k experts for each token， activating only a sparse subset of the total parameters. The post provides examples like Mixtral 8×7B and DeepSeek-V3， showing how MoE enables massive parameter scaling while keeping compute per token low. It also discusses training challenges such as expert imbalance， solved via load balancing losses or dynamic routing bias. Finally， it highlights engineering trade-offs in expert count， communication overhead， and capacity factors.

💡 主要观点

  1. MoE uses sparse activation to scale parameters without proportional compute increase Only the top-k experts are activated per token， so while total parameters can grow to 671B， actual compute per token remains around 37B as in DeepSeek-V3， making it efficient compared to dense models.
  2. Load balancing is critical to prevent expert imbalance during training Without balancing， some experts may dominate; a loss term like L_balance ensures both expert utilization and router preference remain uniform， or dynamic bias can correct routing without auxiliary loss.
  3. Engineering trade-offs exist in expert count and capacity factor More experts increase all-to-all communication and memory usage; capacity factor must be tuned to avoid dropping tokens (too low) or wasting compute (too high)， and expert specialization is statistical， not semantic.
