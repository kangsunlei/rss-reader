RL Training Experiment: Fine-tuning Qwen3-1.7B to Avoid Letter 'e'

11/7/2026, 2:18:33 AM

📌 一句话摘要

A hands-on RL experiment details how to train a small language model to avoid using the letter 'e'， using SFT and RLHF techniques with novel prompts and rewards to achieve 60.5% accuracy.

📝 详细摘要

The tweet shares a practical RL training experiment conducted by Kevin Wu on a 16GB GPU， adapting Qwen3-1.7B to answer questions without using the letter 'e'. Initial attempts with only instruction tuning yielded 0% accuracy. A logits mask improved results to 26% but caused coherence issues. Through supervised fine-tuning on custom data (180 e-free Q&A pairs) and subsequent reinforcement learning using GPT-6 Luna as a reward model， accuracy reached 60.5%. The process demonstrates the importance of domain-specific data and careful reward design. Trade-offs include shorter responses (median tokens dropped from 53 to 24).

💡 主要观点

  1. Initial baseline with instruction tuning achieved 0% accuracy on avoiding 'e'. This shows the inherent linguistic bias of language models and the difficulty of enforcing strict formatting constraints through prompts alone.
  2. Logits masking improved accuracy to 26% but degraded coherence. While masking prevents token sampling， it can cause the model to generate repetitive or nonsensical outputs when the vocabulary is constrained.
  3. Supervised fine-tuning on custom e-free Q&A pairs raised accuracy to 35%. Domain-specific training data proved critical in teaching the model to maintain both constraint adherence and answerability.
  4. RL with GPT-6 Luna as a reward model achieved 60.5% accuracy via GRPO training. Reinforcement learning from human-aligned rewards enabled better balance between linguistic constraints and response quality through iterative optimization.
  5. Response length decreased significantly as a trade-off. The constraint of avoiding common letters like 'e' forced shorter outputs， highlighting the cost of extreme prompt constraints on expression richness.

💬 文章金句

  • Kevin Wu on his 16GB GPU trained Qwen3-1.7B to answer questions without using the letter 'e'， a small RL experiment course.
  • Only with instructions， 200 questions had 0% validity. With 'logits mask'， up to 26%， but the model started speaking nonsense， writing 'riddiculous riddiculous'.
  • The real progress came in two steps. SFT: training on the full e-free novel Gadsby didn't help， but my own 180 e-free Q&A pairs raised it to 35%.
  • Then RL: using GPT-6 Luna as a judge， scoring coherence and relevance from 1 to 5， GRPO trained for 300 steps， raising validity from 35% to 60.5%.
  • The difficulty isn't avoiding 'e'， it's speaking well with the remaining words. The cost is that answers get shorter， median tokens from 53 to 24. But on-target data is worth more than off-target data.
