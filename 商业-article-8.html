P-H3-Image Hardcore Real-World Test: Third-Person Immersive Scene Composition Analysis

📌 一句话摘要

A technical evaluation of P-H3-Image's ability to generate immersive first-person perspective scene compositions based on a conceptual ship-riding hamster illustration， revealing both its visual quality and limitations in character consistency and spatial continuity.

📝 详细摘要

The author conducted an experimental test of P-H3-Image， attempting to bring to life a whimsical concept—a mechanical hamster riding a bicycle. Results show that the model excels at producing visually striking， immersive first-person perspective scene compositions and concept shots with strong aesthetic appeal. However， it exhibits minor fluctuations in character traits， lacks strict spatial continuity， requires rework for character repetition， and occasionally produces staged expressions， skin textures， and movements. Performance comparisons indicate that Qwen Image 2.1 demands more nuanced prompt engineering for similar outputs. Technical specs include RTX 5080 16GB + 32GB RAM， 8-step Euler sampling at ~4MP resolution， with timing data showing median 133.13 seconds per image (range: 128.65–513.67 seconds)， plus 116.79 seconds for post-processing correction.

💡 主要观点

  1. P-H3-Image produces visually compelling first-person perspective scene compositions and concept shots. The test demonstration showed that the model's output has strong aesthetic value and immersion， making it suitable for creating detailed concept art and cinematic storyboards with a sense of presence.
  2. Character consistency and spatial continuity remain problematic for multi-frame sequences. Issues such as character trait fluctuations， non-strict geometric continuity， and ship drift in multi-shot scenarios require manual correction， limiting its use in strictly sequential video storyboards.
  3. Prompt optimization for competing models like Qwen Image 2.1 is more demanding. Compared to P-H3-Image， Qwen Image 2.1 requires significantly more effort in crafting precise prompts to achieve comparable results， highlighting the importance of prompt engineering in AI image generation workflows.
