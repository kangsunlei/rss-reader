Dwarkesh: What does the next training paradigm look like?

6/29/2026, 11:00:00 PM

📌 一句话摘要

Dwarkesh Patel delivers a high-density solo thesis dissecting the fundamental flaws of current AI training and outlining why continuous learning from real-world deployment is the essential path to general intelligence.

📝 详细摘要

In this solo audio essay， Dwarkesh Patel argues that the prevailing Reinforcement Learning from Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) paradigm is insufficient for achieving truly general intelligence. He identifies the current bottleneck not just as data inefficiency but as a lack of "honeability"—the ability to build deterministic， replayable simulators for complex real-world skills like entrepreneurship or politics. Patel systematically builds the case that AI must evolve from a "classroom genius" to a "workplace elite" through continuous on-the-job learning， where models update their weights from scarce， high-signal real-world interactions. He introduces key technical concepts like "on-policy self-distillation" as a solution superior to naive supervision， and speculates on a fourth scaling dimension called "dreaming，" where models build internal simulators for rehearsal. The essay concludes with a 2027-2028 scenario where an RLVR-trained agent is deployed broadly， and its primary mode of progress shifts entirely to learning from billions of global interactions， making the model smarter with every user query.

💡 主要观点

  1. "Verifiability" is not enough; a field also needs "honeability" for RL-based training to work. Progress in fields like computer use is slow because you can't easily create a deterministic， parallelizable simulator for it. Skills like founding a company or running a political campaign lack this property entirely， exposing a deep limit of current RL paradigms.
  2. Continuous， on-the-job weight updates are critical for models to master non-ergodic， high-stakes real-world tasks. The most valuable training data—an organization's implicit knowledge， specific failure modes—only appears during deployment. Like a genius grad student who never interns， models waste this information if they can't learn from it in an ongoing way.
  3. On-Policy Self-Distillation (OPSD) is a superior method for continuous learning than behavioral cloning. OPSD distills the knowledge a model gains in a long context window back into its base weights. It provides a dense， token-level supervisory signal and only extracts the minimal necessary updates， avoiding the catastrophic forgetting that comes from replaying every detail.
  4. Sample efficiency， not just scale， is the key to unlocking general skills. Since many real-world domains provide only sparse， unique data points， models must become radically more sample-efficient. This can be achieved by compressing high-signal experiences into tiny， precise weight updates and potentially through 'dreaming'—training inside AI-generated simulators.

💬 文章金句

  • We're like a genius graduate student who never gets to actually do an internship. We're just giving them more and more classroom case studies within the RL environment training.
  • A field having verifiability is not enough. It must also have high honeability.
  • The human emphasis on continuous learning is not about having everything you've ever observed on the tip of your tongue; more about chiseling the right intuitions and global knowledge back into the weights.
  • RL learns less per sample than supervised learning... this might be a good thing， not a bad thing. You're only making the most necessary changes to the model to achieve an outcome—nothing more.
  • Every time you interact with an AI， it gets smarter... not just because it learned from your previous session， but because it learned from all the interactions all other users are having around the world.
